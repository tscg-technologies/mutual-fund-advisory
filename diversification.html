The Art & Science of Diversification

Learn how to build a diversified portfolio across asset classes, sectors, and cap sizes without falling into over-diversification.

Core Principle

The Only Free Lunch in Finance

Nobel laureate Harry Markowitz famously described diversification as the only free lunch in finance. By combining assets whose returns do not move in perfect tandem (imperfect or negative correlation), an investor can reduce overall portfolio volatility without sacrificing long-term expected returns.

A well-constructed portfolio achieves diversification on three levels:

  • 1. Asset Class Diversification: Spreading wealth across Equities, Fixed Income, and Gold.
  • 2. Market Cap Diversification: Blending Large, Mid, and Small Cap company exposure.
  • 3. Sector & Geographic Diversification: Ensuring no single economic sector accounts for an outsized portion of assets.
Common Pitfall

The Trap of Over-Diversification

Why owning 25 mutual funds rarely performs better than owning 4 to 6 well-chosen schemes.

Portfolio Dilution

Holding dozens of schemes turns your active portfolio into an expensive proxy for the index, while charging active management fees.

Stock Overlap

Five large-cap schemes from different AMCs will likely hold 70-80% of the exact same underlying top 30 companies.

Administrative Friction

Managing capital gains statements, tax filings, and performance tracking becomes needlessly complex and unmanageable.
