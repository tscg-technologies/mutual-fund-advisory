Fund of Funds (FoF)

A Fund of Funds (FoF) is a mutual fund scheme that invests its assets in units of other mutual fund schemes, rather than directly holding individual shares or bonds.

Category Definition & Mechanics

How Fund of Funds (FoF) Function

The FoF manager selects a basket of high-performing underlying schemes across various AMCs or asset classes. For example, a Gold FoF invests in Gold ETFs, while an Asset Allocation FoF might hold 60% in an equity fund and 40% in a debt fund.

Primary Objective: To provide convenient single-ticket access to a diversified basket of underlying managers, specialized asset classes (like gold), or overseas strategies.

Regulatory Risk Disclosure: Investments in Fund of Funds (FoF) are subject to market risks. Read all scheme information documents (SID) and key information memorandums (KIM) carefully before committing capital.
Category Blueprint

Operational Characteristics

Parameter Category Standard & SEBI Framework
General Risk Rating Dependent on the underlying funds. An equity FoF is High Risk, while a debt FoF is Moderate Risk.
Recommended Investment Horizon 3 to 5+ years depending on the underlying asset composition.
Portfolio Construction Holding list comprises other mutual fund scheme unit holdings rather than individual company stocks.
Key Risks to Monitor Double expense ratio impact (the FoF charges an expense, and the underlying schemes also deduct their own TERs, subject to SEBI aggregate caps).
Costs & Liquidity Profile SEBI regulates total combined expense caps. Settlement may take an extra day (T+3) because underlying scheme units must be liquidated first.

Questions to Ask Before Investing

  • • What is the total combined expense ratio of the FoF and its underlying schemes?
  • • Could I achieve the same asset allocation more cost-effectively by investing in the underlying funds directly?
  • • What is the tax status of this specific FoF scheme?
